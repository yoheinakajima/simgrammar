# Brief: read every panel sheet a second time, in one run

**Run kind:** `verification`. **Size:** the whole panel that `report/analysis.yaml` records under `second_coding` and `panel`: the three coding sheets of `tasks/second-coding.md`, technique recall for 30 mechanics, maturity, TRL and horizon for every technique, the six rubric scales for 40 mechanics after the calibration set, and novelty, feasibility and impact for every opportunity. The PR adds your run file and codes files, and adds ratings to about 80 records, so it needs the maintainer's `bulk` label (E039).

**Who can take it:** an invited contributor (the repository's owner, a member, or a collaborator) whose operator did not create or review the records, using a different model from theirs if an agent does the reading. Most records were made by runs of `yoheinakajima` with `claude-opus-5-5`. The packet leaves out any item your run may not read, so you never fill what the recorder would refuse.

Most of what the paper concludes rests on judgments one rater made once: a mechanic's family and admission, each technique edge and its effect, each technique's maturity, TRL and horizon, and every rubric score. The panel turns them into measured agreement (review findings D3, D4, D6, D8, E2, E17, E31):

| Sheet | You give | The build reports |
|---|---|---|
| admission, family, effect | pass or fail on four tests, a family, an effect | Cohen's kappa and Krippendorff's alpha per decision (`second-coding-agreement.csv`) |
| recall | for each sampled mechanic, every technique that changes it, with an effect | recall of the recorded edges, the edges you add, effect agreement on the edges you both list, and catalog recall with an interval over mechanics (`second-coding-panel-recall.csv`) |
| maturity | maturity, TRL, horizon for every technique | nominal kappa and alpha for maturity and horizon, quadratic kappa and ordinal alpha for TRL (`second-coding-panel-codes.csv`, `-ratings.csv`) |
| calibration, rubric | the six mechanic scales | your calibration agreement (E036), then ordinal alpha and weighted kappa per scale and the §5.6 correlations under your scores (`second-coding-panel-rubric-correlations.csv`, `rater-agreement.csv`) |
| opportunity | novelty, feasibility, impact | agreement per score, the rank correlation of the two priority composites, the overlap of the top 15, and the ranking the lower median gives (`second-coding-panel-opportunity-ranking.csv`) |

1. **Open your run first:** `python scripts/new.py run <slug> --kind verification --actor agent --operator <your-handle> --model <model-id> --harness "<tool>" --purpose "<one sentence>"`.
2. **Get the packet:** `python scripts/panel.py packet --run <run-id>` writes `panel-out/` (gitignored): `INSTRUCTIONS.md`, `manifest.yaml`, and the sheets, numbered in the order to fill them. Each sheet gives the written rules or anchors, the vocabulary's definitions, and each item's own text, and none gives a stored code or score. A coder with nothing but this directory can fill it, so you can hand it to an agent with no repository and no web access.
3. **Fill it blind, in file order, once.** Do not open `data/`, `log/`, `report/`, `dist/`, `book/`, `media/`, the website, or the web until every sheet is filled: they show the current codes and scores, and nothing but your procedure keeps the reading blind. The order matters: the family sheet lists only admitted mechanics, the effect sheet shows edges the recall sheet asks you to find, and the opportunity sheet pairs mechanics with techniques. Score the calibration file before the rubric sheet. Fill every `null` as `INSTRUCTIONS.md` says, with a rationale for every answer, and keep what you wrote: do not compare with the stored values and redo a sheet.
4. **Record:** `python scripts/panel.py record panel-out --run <run-id> --dry-run` checks everything and names every problem; fix exactly those, then run it without `--dry-run`. It writes:
   - admission, family and effect codes to `report/coding/<run-id>.yaml`, in the format of `tasks/second-coding.md`;
   - recall codes to `report/coding/recall/<run-id>.yaml`, and maturity and horizon codes to `report/coding/maturity/<run-id>.yaml`;
   - your calibration into your run file, as `calibrate.py score --write-run` does;
   - your scores into the records as `ratings` (tasks/rate-scores.md): the creator's original score as a rating when the field has none yet, your rating with its rationale, the lower median of all of them stored, and your run appended to `provenance.reviewed`. Mechanic ratings are written only when your calibration passes (E036); otherwise recalibrate in a new run.
   - TRL readings stay in the maturity codes file, with your other technique codes: a record's `ratings` hold values up to 5 (`schema/common.schema.json`), and TRL runs to 9, so a TRL rating cannot be stored in a record until the schema allows it.

   Nothing is written if any part fails, and a second record by the same operator and model is refused: one rater, one reading.
5. **Check:** `make build && make check`. Expect assertion failures (E029, E032): §2.6 asserts that no codes have been collected, and a stored score that moves to a lower median can move any sentence that ranks or correlates scores. That is the expected result. Do not edit `report/src/`, `book/`, or `media/`: quote the errors in the PR, and the maintainer rewrites the sentences with the agreement the build now reports (AGENTS.md §8). Everything else must be green.
6. **Commit and open the PR** that links the issue: the run file, the codes files, and the changed records (`panel-out/` is gitignored). Say in the run's `method` which sheets you filled, in what order, how long it took, and anything you opened before you finished. Ask for the `bulk` label; sign off every commit after reading the diff.

**A cross-model check is not this task.** The maintainer can run another model on the same packet under his own operator (`python scripts/panel.py packet --run <run-id> --cross-model`, a `maintenance` run whose model differs from every run that made or reviewed the items). It records to `report/cross-model/<run-id>.yaml`, changes no record, and the paper reports it as a check across models (`xm_*` stats, `cross-model-*.csv`), never as independent agreement. Only a run like yours, by a different operator, counts as a second rater (CONTRACT.md I8, I14, E035).
