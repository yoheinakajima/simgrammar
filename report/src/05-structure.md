### 5.4 Families of games by what they use

Clustering games by the mechanics they use, ignoring their subgenre labels, groups the catalog into {{stat:n_clusters}} clusters at the configured cut. Games are joined by average linkage on the weighted Jaccard distance between their mechanic sets. Each mechanic is weighted by inverse prevalence, so two games sharing a rare mechanic count as closer than two games sharing a pause button.

{{viz:design-map}}

{{table:game-clusters}}

The number of clusters is not tuned to the result. The build re-clusters {{stat:cluster_subsamples}} subsamples, each holding about {{stat:cluster_subsample_pct}} percent of the games, and compares each with the full clustering by adjusted Rand index (ARI). A game's membership in each subsample is fixed by a hash of its id, so adding or removing a game leaves the other games' subsamples unchanged. The rule takes the smallest k whose mean ARI reaches {{stat:cluster_min_mean_ari}}. When it was last applied it selected k = {{stat:cluster_k}} (mean ARI now {{stat:cluster_mean_ari}}), and that k is fixed in `report/analysis.yaml` so that adding a game does not reshuffle every cluster. On the current data the rule would select k = {{stat:cluster_rule_k}}; the table marks the configured k and the rule's choice, one row when they agree. The validator warns only when the two differ by more than resampling noise ({{stat:cluster_drift_tolerance}} in mean ARI), and they do not.{{assert:cluster_k_drift == 0}} The curve is flat around the threshold (figure): every k from {{stat:cluster_k_near_min}} to {{stat:cluster_k}} comes within that noise of it, a few added games moved the rule's pick by several clusters, and with a threshold of {{stat:cluster_min_mean_ari_lo}} or {{stat:cluster_min_mean_ari_hi}} the rule would pick k = {{stat:cluster_rule_k_lo}} or {{stat:cluster_rule_k_hi}}.{{assert:cluster_k_near_min < cluster_k}}{{assert:cluster_rule_k_lo < cluster_k}}{{assert:cluster_rule_k_hi > cluster_k}} The {{stat:cluster_k}}-way cut is therefore one admissible partition among its neighbours. The claims below are about groupings that survive resampling, other cuts, and other methods.

{{table:cluster-stability}}

The stability column of the cluster table follows Hennig's subsetting scheme [@src-hennig-2026-fpc-clusterboot]. In each subsample, a cluster is restricted to the games the subsample keeps and compared with every cluster of the subsample's own clustering by Jaccard overlap (games in both over games in either), and the best match counts. The mean runs over the subsamples that keep at least one of the cluster's games; a subsample that keeps a single one scores 1 only when that game is alone in its subsample cluster. Stability therefore measures how the kept games regroup. Measured against the whole cluster, with the games a subsample drops counted as missed, even perfect recovery would score only about the subsample fraction, and the highest cluster reaches {{stat:cluster_full_membership_max}} on that measure.{{assert:cluster_full_membership_max < 0.85}} Hennig's guidance takes a mean of {{stat:cluster_stable_jaccard}} or more as the mark of a stable cluster, {{stat:cluster_stable_jaccard_lo}} to {{stat:cluster_stable_jaccard}} as a pattern whose exact members are doubtful, and {{stat:cluster_stable_jaccard_hi}} or more as highly stable [@src-hennig-2026-fpc-clusterboot]. This section also requires a stable cluster to hold at least {{stat:cluster_min_family_size}} games, because a pair or a trio is better described by how often its games share a cluster. {{stat:n_stable_clusters}} of the {{stat:n_clusters}} clusters pass both tests and {{stat:n_stable_clusters_any_size}} pass the overlap test alone; at Hennig's lower and upper marks, {{stat:n_stable_clusters_jaccard_lo}} and {{stat:n_stable_clusters_jaccard_hi}} clusters of that size pass.{{assert:n_stable_clusters < n_stable_clusters_any_size}}{{assert:n_stable_clusters < n_clusters}} When this section says games belong together, they share a cluster in at least {{stat:cocluster_together_pct}} percent of subsamples; when it says they stand apart, in at most {{stat:cocluster_apart_pct}} percent. Weaker links are described as weaker and pinned by comparison. The build fails if any of these stops holding.

### 5.6 The legibility frontier

Mechanics that generate more unscripted variety are rated harder to read. Across all mechanics, legibility and emergence potential are negatively correlated (Spearman ρ = {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential}}, {{stat:pct_bootstrap_interval}} percent interval {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_family_low}} to {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_family_high}} when whole families are resampled), emergence potential rises with tuning difficulty (ρ = {{stat:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty}}, {{stat:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty_family_low}} to {{stat:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty_family_high}}), and legibility falls as compute cost rises (ρ = {{stat:rho_legibility_compute_cost}}, {{stat:rho_legibility_compute_cost_family_low}} to {{stat:rho_legibility_compute_cost_family_high}}).{{assert:rho_legibility_emergence_potential < -0.2}}{{assert:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty > 0.2}}{{assert:rho_legibility_compute_cost < 0}} In these scores, the mechanics that feed more variety into a game are also the harder ones to understand and to balance, and the costlier ones to run are harder to read.
