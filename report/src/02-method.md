## 2. Method

### 2.1 Unit of analysis: the mechanic

A **mechanic** here is a rule-system that passes four admission tests (CONTRACT.md, I12):

- **Nameable.** Designers would recognize it in games other than the one being examined.
- **Atomic.** It owns one kind of state and one update rule. If it splits into two parts that each appear without the other in shipped games, it is two mechanics.
- **Tunable.** It has at least two levers.
- **Removable.** A game could drop it and keep running.

Interface mechanics pass the atomic test on presentation state. An inspector owns its selected agent and the fields it exposes ([[agent-inspection]]), an alert feed owns its list of active alerts with their severities and lifetimes ([[alerts-feed]]), and an overlay owns which map mode is shown and the color ramp it draws through ([[data-overlays]]); each updates that state from simulation state that other mechanics own, which is the sense in which §4.1 says they own no simulation state of their own.{{assert:field.agent-inspection.family == information-legibility}}{{assert:field.alerts-feed.family == information-legibility}}{{assert:field.data-overlays.family == information-legibility}}

The tests exclude two common confusions. Dynamics such as "cascading failure" or "death spiral" are outcomes of coupled mechanics, so they appear in mechanics' `dynamics` fields rather than as entities. Genre-level patterns such as "colony management" are combinations, so they appear as game decompositions or opportunities. The result sits between the MDA framework's *mechanics* layer and a designer's pattern language: concrete enough to implement, general enough to recur (§1.1).

Each mechanic belongs to one of {{stat:n_families}} families, assigned by the state it owns rather than the genre it appears in: [[weather]] is environment, [[labor-market]] is economy, [[agent-inspection]] is information.

### 2.2 Corpus

The game catalog was built to cover every subgenre in the vocabulary, every lineage-defining title from the 1960s onward, recent releases through {{stat:year_max}}, research demonstrations that run as interactive simulations (such as [[generative-agents-smallville]] and [[project-sid]]), and works included for a single mechanic (marked `boundary_case`; {{stat:n_boundary_cases}} such works). The catalog is a purposive sample, not a census: prevalence figures describe this corpus, and the earliest-appearance year of a mechanic is a lower bound on its true origin.

### 2.3 Schema and scoring

Every categorical field draws from a closed vocabulary (`vocab/`), and every ordinal field names an anchored rubric (`vocab/rubrics.yaml`). Scorers match the anchor text and take the lower anchor when between two. This trades resolution for consistency across contributors: a 3 in legibility means "understood after deliberate attention and the game's info tools" whoever assigned it. The lower-anchor rule makes each score a claim that the mechanic reaches at least that anchor, and the cost is that a score sits below the scorer's best guess wherever the text falls between two anchors. The original scores were assigned by one model rater (claude-opus-5-5) matching the anchor text. Independent second ratings now cover {{stat:n_scores_independently_rated}} scores from {{stat:n_mechanics_independently_rated}} mechanics; fields with ratings store their lower median, which for two ratings is the lower one, so stored values are conservative in the same direction as the anchor rule. §5.6 reports what storing the mean or the upper rating would change.{{assert:n_scores_independently_rated > 0}} Scores remain judgments, and the rubric for each is reproduced in the wiki.

Two scales share a word. A game's `emergence` score (§3) is anchored on what players report about the whole game, up to histories no one could have authored. A mechanic's `emergence_potential` score is anchored on what the mechanic produces "when coupled with others", up to "a primary engine of emergent stories wherever it appears", so a rater judges it from how the mechanic behaves alongside others in the games that use it. It rates a mechanic's typical contribution in context, not a property the mechanic has on its own, and emergence potential in this paper always means the mechanic score.

Relations are typed edges with one owner each (`vocab/edge_types.yaml`). Inverses (which games use a mechanic, which techniques affect it) and all aggregates (prevalence, earliest appearance, clusters, technique exposure, opportunity priority) are computed by the build and never stored, so they cannot drift from the edges they summarize.

### 2.4 Evidence

Each entity carries an epistemic status. `asserted` means written from domain knowledge without a citation; `sourced` means at least one cited source supports its factual fields; `verified` requires a second, independent run. Techniques must always cite. At this version, {{stat:n_sourced_or_verified}} entities are sourced or verified, {{stat:n_contested}} is contested, with both sides cited,{{assert:n_contested == 1}} and {{stat:n_asserted}} remain asserted; the asserted ones form a public verification queue (`dist/analysis/verification-queue.csv`).

### 2.5 Production

The dataset was produced by parallel research runs, each recorded in `log/runs/` with its operator, model, method, and the proposals it could not act on. Every run was an AI agent run directed by a human operator: all {{stat:n_runs}} runs in the log name an agent model, {{stat:n_runs_top_model}} of them {{stat:top_run_model}} and the rest other models ({{stat:n_run_models}} models in all), under {{stat:n_run_operators}} operators.{{assert:n_runs_agent == n_runs}}{{assert:n_runs_top_model <= n_runs}} Parallel runs partitioned work by file, not by topic: taxonomy runs analyzed mechanics; catalog runs verified release facts against public sources and decomposed games; technique runs searched the 2019 to 2026 literature and developer announcements. Merge-time runs then edited entities across partitions: the synthesis run added symmetric synergy and tension edges in one pass, and a review run added mechanic tags to games after an independent review found widely shared mechanics (pause controls, placement, tech trees) under-tagged, recoded several decompositions, and tightened every prediction. Each cross-partition edit is attributed in the entity's `provenance.reviewed` list. A validator enforcing the rules in CONTRACT.md §5 gated every merge. The same procedure (AGENTS.md) is what a new contributor, human or agent, follows.

### 2.6 Limitations

- **Sampling.** Commercial, English-language, PC-centric titles are over-represented; mobile management games and non-Western simulation traditions are under-represented.
- **Scoring.** Most rubric scores remain judgments by the original model (claude-opus-5-5), with an independently rated subset. They are not measurements of player behavior. Correlations among scores (§5.6) and between a score and the technique graph (§8.4) partly reflect that rater's internal consistency. Each score can carry `ratings` from further raters, each with a run and a rationale; the original score counts as one of them, and the stored value is their lower median (E037). A rater must come from a different operator, and a different model for agents (E035), and must first agree with a calibration set of {{stat:n_calibration_mechanics}} mechanics (E036). The calibration's reference scores are public, so it shows that a rater reads the anchors as the set does, not that the rater scored blind. {{stat:n_scores_independently_rated}} of the {{stat:n_mechanic_scores}} mechanic scores have a second, independent rating so far.
- **The second rater.** One rater has added ratings so far:{{assert:n_rating_runs == 1}} a model (gpt-6.1-sol) under a different operator, in run `2026-10-29-shreyko-antim-rate-scores-19`. By that run's record, the rater first scored the calibration sheet, which gives each calibration mechanic's summary, definition, player experience, and levers with the anchors and no scores, and fixed those scores before they were compared with the reference. It then scored the {{stat:n_mechanics_independently_rated}} mechanics of its task from a packet of their definitions, player experience, levers, failure modes, and dynamics, again without stored scores, earlier ratings, rationales, or provenance. That account is the operator's: the reference scores are public, and nothing in the data shows that the rater did not see them. Two facts the records do establish limit what the agreement shows. Both raters read descriptions written by the original rater in the same runs that assigned its scores, so a judgment carried in that text, such as a failure mode saying players misread a hazard, reaches both; the agreement in §5.6 is therefore an upper bound on what fully independent raters would reach. And the rated mechanics were not sampled: they are the first batch the task generator (`scripts/make_tasks.py`) handed out, the mechanics outside the calibration set taken in alphabetical order of id, and the operator claimed that task, so the agreement may not hold for the rest of the vocabulary. Ratings on a sample stratified by family and score and several times larger, from raters who write their own descriptions or score from play, need new raters and are not part of this version.
- **Decomposition depth.** A game's mechanic list records what is present and meaningful, not a complete specification. Absence of an edge is weak evidence of absence.
- **Recency.** Technique maturity and horizon are judged as of the run date and age quickly; each technique records the sources the judgment rests on.
