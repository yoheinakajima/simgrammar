## 2. Method

### 2.1 Unit of analysis: the mechanic

A **mechanic** here is a rule-system that passes four admission tests (CONTRACT.md, I12). Each test has a procedure. The validator enforces a threshold for two of them; the other two are the writer's judgment.

- **Nameable.** Designers would recognize it in games other than the one being examined. The name describes the rule rather than one title's term for it, and a mechanic whose maturity is classic or established must be tagged on at least two catalog games (E017); only emerging and experimental mechanics may sit on one, and {{stat:n_mechanics_one_game}} do.
- **Atomic.** It owns one kind of state and one update rule, which its `definition` names. If it splits into two parts that each appear without the other in shipped games, it is two mechanics, and naming two such games is what a split must show. The writer makes this call from knowledge of games, and no mechanic has since been merged into another ({{stat:n_mechanic_deprecated}} deprecated). Counts of mechanics, such as the post-2010 count in §5.2, are at the grain this test sets.{{assert:n_mechanic_deprecated == 0}}
- **Tunable.** It has at least two levers. The schema requires at least two entries in `design_levers`; every mechanic lists at least {{stat:min_design_levers}}, and the median is {{stat:median_design_levers}}.
- **Removable.** A game could drop it and keep running. The build checks that no mechanic is in every catalog game: the most used, {{stat:most_prevalent_mechanic}}, is in {{stat:most_prevalent_mechanic_share_pct}} percent of them.{{assert:most_prevalent_mechanic_share_pct < 100}}

The atomic and nameable tests do the rejecting. Tunable and removable filter little in practice, and the second model of Appendix F failed no item on either.{{assert:xm_n_admission_fail_tunable == 0}}{{assert:xm_n_admission_fail_removable == 0}}

Interface mechanics pass the atomic test on presentation state. An inspector owns its selected agent and the fields it exposes ([[agent-inspection]]), an alert feed owns its list of active alerts with their severities and lifetimes ([[alerts-feed]]), and an overlay owns which map mode is shown and the color ramp it draws through ([[data-overlays]]); each updates that state from simulation state that other mechanics own, which is the sense in which §4.1 says they own no simulation state of their own.{{assert:field.agent-inspection.family == information-legibility}}{{assert:field.alerts-feed.family == information-legibility}}{{assert:field.data-overlays.family == information-legibility}} Control mechanics pass it on a command channel, state that persists between the player's actions and that agents or machines consume (§1.1). The tests therefore admit presentation, control, and clock mechanics beside the ones that own simulation state.

So each mechanic is also given a state class, by what its update rule writes, and the analyses that count mechanics are re-run on the ones that model a world (§5.1, §5.3, §5.4). **Simulation state** is a variable of the modelled world that the rule computes from other world variables, from chance, or from elapsed time, and that other rules read. **Control** is the player's means of acting (orders, programs, settings, structures placed where the player puts them, a body the player steers, a pool only the player spends) and the pace of simulated time. **Meta-structure** is the frame around play: goals, scores, unlocks, the session, and content from outside it. **Presentation** is what the player is shown. A mechanic that writes variables of more than one class takes the first of them in that order, so simulation state wins a tie. Membership of a second class is not recorded, so the simulation-state subset is the mechanics whose rule writes at least one world variable, some of which also drive the interface or a control channel, and the counts of the other classes are lower bounds. Appendix E gives the rules in full with two worked examples per class, and `report/state-class.yaml` gives every mechanic's class, with a one-line rationale for the {{stat:n_state_class_why}} calls the rules do not settle at a glance; the build fails if a live mechanic has no class. {{stat:n_state_class_simulation_state}} of the {{count:mechanic}} mechanics are simulation state, and they carry {{stat:pct_uses_state_class_simulation_state}} percent of all uses; {{stat:n_state_class_control}} are control, {{stat:n_state_class_meta_structure}} meta-structure, and {{stat:n_state_class_presentation}} presentation.{{assert:n_state_class_simulation_state > n_state_class_nonstate}}{{assert:n_state_class_control > n_state_class_meta_structure}}{{assert:n_state_class_meta_structure > n_state_class_presentation}} The classes do not follow the families: building placement is control and modding support meta-structure, in families whose other mechanics mostly model the world, and §5.1 compares the classes with the family-level proxy used before them.{{assert:n_state_class_proxy_keeps_nonstate > 0}}{{assert:n_state_class_proxy_drops_state > 0}}

The tests exclude two common confusions. Dynamics such as "cascading failure" or "death spiral" are outcomes of coupled mechanics, so they appear in mechanics' `dynamics` fields rather than as entities. Genre-level patterns such as "colony management" are combinations, so they appear as game decompositions or opportunities. The result sits between the MDA framework's *mechanics* layer and a designer's pattern language: concrete enough to implement, general enough to recur (§1.1).

Each mechanic belongs to one of {{stat:n_families}} families, assigned by the state it owns rather than the genre it appears in, against the family definitions in `vocab/mechanic_family.yaml`. The rules apply in order, and the first that fits decides:

1. A mechanic whose update rule writes only what the player is shown is information and legibility ([[agent-inspection]]). One that writes the simulation clock or its rate is time and pacing ([[variable-speed]]). One that writes a standing channel from player intent to action is control and automation ([[designation-orders]]).
2. Otherwise the family is the one whose definition names the variable the update rule stores between ticks: [[weather]] is environment, [[labor-market]] is economy. How the player reaches the mechanic does not decide its family; that is the `player_interface` field. [[natural-language-interaction]] takes free text, but the state it owns is a character's interpretation of the utterance and reply, so it is social relations; [[player-legislation]] writes laws, so it is governance.
3. When a rule writes one variable from another, the stored variable decides. [[supply-demand-pricing]] stores prices computed from stocks of goods, so it is economy; [[goods-consumption]] draws down stocks of goods and passes the share of needs met to other mechanics, so it is resources and production.
4. A value held inside one agent is agents and behavior ([[needs-decay]]); a value attached to a pair or group of agents is social relations ([[relationship-values]], [[reputation]]); a count over many agents is population and lineage ([[population-strata]]).
5. Moving goods along a route is logistics ([[conveyor-transport]]) unless a price difference drives the exchange, which makes it economy ([[trade-routes]]).{{assert:field.variable-speed.family == time-pacing}}{{assert:field.designation-orders.family == control-automation}}{{assert:field.natural-language-interaction.family == social-relations}}{{assert:field.player-legislation.family == governance-policy}}{{assert:field.supply-demand-pricing.family == economy-finance}}{{assert:field.goods-consumption.family == resources-production}}{{assert:field.needs-decay.family == agents-behavior}}{{assert:field.relationship-values.family == social-relations}}{{assert:field.reputation.family == social-relations}}{{assert:field.population-strata.family == population-lineage}}{{assert:field.conveyor-transport.family == logistics-networks}}{{assert:field.trade-routes.family == economy-finance}}

The run that created each mechanic assigned its family and its admission, and the family rules above were written from those assignments. The state classes came later, in one pass, with every class fixed before any statistic on the subset was computed. A second model coding a sample blind from these rules and each mechanic's own text (Appendix F) gave the stored family to {{stat:xm_pct_agree_family}} percent of the sampled mechanics (κ = {{stat:xm_kappa_family}}, {{stat:xm_kappa_family_low}} to {{stat:xm_kappa_family_high}}) and the stored state class to {{stat:xm_pct_agree_state}} percent (κ = {{stat:xm_kappa_state}}): a reader with different training follows the written rules to the same codes. The rules were written from the assignments and are applied to text from the same runs, so the agreement shows that the families can be reproduced, not that they are the right ones. §8.2 measures how far the findings move when mechanics whose wording fits two families change family.{{assert:xm_kappa_family >= 0.8}}{{assert:xm_kappa_state >= 0.7}} On admission the second model agreed with the dataset's boundary on {{stat:xm_pct_agree_admission}} percent of the items (κ = {{stat:xm_kappa_admission}}, low because nearly every item passes; prevalence-adjusted agreement {{stat:xm_pabak_admission}}). It kept {{stat:xm_n_admission_admitted_kept}} of the {{stat:xm_n_admission_admitted}} admitted mechanics in the sample and rejected {{stat:xm_n_admission_negatives_rejected}} of the {{stat:xm_n_admission_negatives}} proposed mechanics the vocabulary has not taken up, failing items on the atomic test ({{stat:xm_n_admission_fail_atomic}}) and the nameable test ({{stat:xm_n_admission_fail_nameable}}). The boundary is a generous one: as written, the tests admit most proposed mechanics, including ones the vocabulary has not taken up.{{assert:xm_kappa_admission < 0.4}}{{assert:xm_n_admission_negatives_rejected < 4}}{{assert:xm_n_admission_fail_atomic > xm_n_admission_fail_nameable}}

### 2.2 Corpus

The game catalog was built to cover every subgenre in the vocabulary, every lineage-defining title from the 1960s onward, recent releases through {{stat:year_max}}, research demonstrations that run as interactive simulations (such as [[generative-agents-smallville]] and [[project-sid]]), and works included for a single mechanic (marked `boundary_case`; {{stat:n_boundary_cases}} such works). The catalog is a purposive sample: prevalence figures describe this corpus, and a mechanic's earliest catalog game gives the latest year it can have originated.

A game's year is the year of its first public release, early access and open betas included, and its mechanics are those of the feature set its sources describe. For a game changed after release, that feature set can include parts added later, so the year can predate some of what the record decomposes; a use note that knows when a feature arrived says so. The records' fields and notes show {{stat:n_games_dating_exposed}} games exposed to this: {{stat:n_games_dating_early_access}} early-access titles (a recorded 1.0 year, or design notes that say early access), {{stat:n_games_dating_persistent_online_world}} persistent online worlds, and {{stat:n_games_dating_later_feature}} games whose use notes date part of a mechanic to a later update, expansion, DLC, or version, some under more than one heading (`dist/analysis/dating-exposed-games.csv`).{{assert:n_games_dating_exposed > 0}} §5.2 recomputes its counts with their uses dated late, to a recorded 1.0 year or a noted later arrival, and without them.

### 2.3 Schema and scoring

Every categorical field draws from a closed vocabulary (`vocab/`), and every ordinal field names an anchored rubric (`vocab/rubrics.yaml`). Scorers match the anchor text and take the lower anchor when between two. This trades resolution for consistency across contributors: a 3 in legibility means "understood after deliberate attention and the game's info tools" whoever assigned it. The lower-anchor rule makes each score a claim that the mechanic reaches at least that anchor, and the cost is that a score sits below the scorer's best guess wherever the text falls between two anchors. The original scores were assigned by one model rater (claude-opus-5-5) matching the anchor text. Independent second ratings now cover {{stat:n_scores_independently_rated}} scores from {{stat:n_mechanics_independently_rated}} mechanics; fields with ratings store their lower median, which for two ratings is the lower one and for three the middle one. Both rules are conservative. Every rating is kept in the record with its rater and run, so the stored value is one view over them, and storing the mean or the upper rating instead moves no correlation in §5.6 by more than {{stat:max_rho_change_aggregation}}.{{assert:n_scores_independently_rated > 0}} The rubric for each score is reproduced in the wiki.

Two scales share a word. A game's `emergence` score (§3) is anchored on what players report about the whole game, up to histories no one could have authored. A mechanic's `emergence_potential` score is anchored on what the mechanic produces "when coupled with others", up to "a primary engine of emergent stories wherever it appears", so a rater judges it from how the mechanic behaves alongside others in the games that use it. It rates a mechanic's typical contribution in context, not a property the mechanic has on its own, and emergence potential in this paper always means the mechanic score.

Relations are typed edges with one owner each (`vocab/edge_types.yaml`). Inverses (which games use a mechanic, which techniques affect it) and all aggregates (prevalence, earliest appearance, clusters, technique exposure, opportunity priority) are computed by the build and never stored, so they cannot drift from the edges they summarize.

### 2.4 Evidence

Each record carries an epistemic status, and the status covers the record's factual fields: a game's release facts and description, a technique's results, a source's claims. A game's decomposition, its core and supporting tags, is the creating run's reading of its sources. The errors a later review found were omissions of widely shared mechanics, which it added (§2.5). Omissions understate prevalence and co-occurrence, most for interface and overlay mechanics, so a pair that is present is stronger evidence than one that is absent. `asserted` means written from domain knowledge and awaiting a source; `sourced` means at least one cited source supports its factual fields; `verified` requires a second, independent run; `contested` means sources disagree, and both sides are cited. Techniques must always cite. At this version, {{stat:n_sourced_or_verified}} of the {{stat:n_records}} records are sourced or verified, {{stat:n_contested}} is contested,{{assert:n_contested == 1}} and {{stat:n_asserted}} remain asserted; the asserted ones form a public verification queue (`dist/analysis/verification-queue.csv`). ### 2.5 Production

The dataset was produced by parallel research runs, each recorded in `log/runs/` with its operator, model, method, and the proposals it could not act on. Every run was an AI agent run directed by a human operator: all {{stat:n_runs}} runs in the log name an agent model, {{stat:n_runs_top_model}} of them {{stat:top_run_model}} and the rest other models ({{stat:n_run_models}} models in all), under {{stat:n_run_operators}} operators.{{assert:n_runs_agent == n_runs}}{{assert:n_runs_top_model <= n_runs}} Parallel runs partitioned work by file, not by topic: taxonomy runs analyzed mechanics; catalog runs verified release facts against public sources and decomposed games; technique runs searched the 2019 to 2026 literature and developer announcements. Merge-time runs then edited entities across partitions: the synthesis run added symmetric synergy and tension edges in one pass, without written criteria for either, and a review run added mechanic tags to games after an independent review found widely shared mechanics (pause controls, placement, tech trees) under-tagged, recoded several decompositions, and tightened every prediction. A later maintenance run, on {{stat:prediction_amendment_date_long}} and before any prediction resolved, wrote each prediction's reference class, generic-diffusion version, and catalog base rate, and added a size bar to the {{stat:n_predictions_amended}} criteria that had none (§11). The baselines are the authors' choices, recorded with the predictions and open to audit (§11.1).{{assert:n_predictions_resolved_by_amendment == 0}} Each cross-partition edit is attributed in the entity's `provenance.reviewed` list. A validator enforcing the rules in CONTRACT.md §5 gated every merge. The same procedure (AGENTS.md) is what a new contributor, human or agent, follows.

The build, drawn in [the figure below](#fig-pipeline), checks three things on every change. It validates every record against the schemas, vocabularies, and contract rules. It fills in every number in the prose from the data, so no count is typed by hand. And it evaluates {{stat:n_report_assertions}} assertions carried by {{stat:n_report_sentences_asserted}} of the paper's {{stat:n_report_sentences}} prose sentences, failing when the data stop supporting the ranking, sign, threshold, grouping, or stored field a sentence states; records carry {{stat:n_record_assertions}} more assertions of the same kind.{{assert:n_report_sentences_asserted < n_report_sentences}} These checks keep the prose consistent with the records: the numbers in the {{stat:n_report_sentences_with_numbers}} sentences that state one agree with the data by construction, and each assertion confirms the sign, rank, or threshold its sentence states. Sources, quotes, and verification runs check the records against the games (§2.4), and verification runs, second ratings, and prediction resolutions must come from a different operator and, when both runs are agents, a different model (E035).

### 2.6 Limitations

The results describe this sample, scored by these raters. Five limits apply throughout, and the sections below name one again only where it changes a specific claim. Appendix F gives the detail.

- **Sample.** The catalog is purposive. Commercial, English-language, PC-centric titles are over-represented, and mobile management games and non-Western simulation traditions are under-represented, so counts describe the catalog.
- **Raters.** Most codes, rubric scores, and opportunity scores come from one model rater (claude-opus-5-5), and {{stat:n_runs_top_model}} of the {{stat:n_runs}} runs used that model.{{assert:n_runs_top_model <= n_runs}} A score is a reading of anchor text, so correlations among scores (§5.6) and between scores and the technique graph (§8.4) are associations in that reading, and an independent second rater has scored {{stat:n_scores_independently_rated}} of the {{stat:n_mechanic_scores}} mechanic scores.{{assert:pct_mechanic_scores_independently_rated < 50}} Coding sheets and a seeded sample are ready for a second person; none has coded yet.{{assert:n_second_coding_runs == 0}}
- **Agreement.** A second model run blind by the same operator reproduces the codes whose rules name a variable or a demonstration: family (κ = {{stat:xm_kappa_family}}), state class ({{stat:xm_kappa_state}}), technique maturity ({{stat:xm_kappa_maturity}}), and TRL (weighted κ = {{stat:xm_kappa_trl}}). It agrees less on horizon ({{stat:xm_kappa_horizon}}) and effect type ({{stat:xm_kappa_effect}}). On the rubric scales its ordinal alpha with the stored scores runs from {{stat:xm_alpha_rubric_min}} on emergence potential to {{stat:xm_alpha_rubric_max}} on legibility, and on the opportunity scores from {{stat:xm_alpha_opportunity_min}} to {{stat:xm_alpha_opportunity_max}}.{{assert:xm_kappa_maturity >= 0.8}}{{assert:xm_kappa_trl >= 0.8}}{{assert:xm_kappa_horizon < xm_kappa_maturity}}{{assert:xm_kappa_effect < xm_kappa_family}}{{assert:xm_alpha_emergence_potential == xm_alpha_rubric_min}}{{assert:xm_alpha_legibility == xm_alpha_rubric_max}}{{assert:xm_alpha_opportunity_max < 0.5}} Each section that rests on one of these decisions reports what the check does to it: §5.6 the score correlations, §7.1 maturity, §8.1 effect types, §8.2 edge recall, §9.1 the ranking.
- **Relations.** The `synergy` and `tension` edges were added by one run in one pass without written criteria, so a pair with no `tension` edge has unmeasured tension (§5.5).
- **Coverage and recency.** A game's mechanic list records what is present and meaningful, and omissions are the commoner tagging error (§2.4). Technique maturity and horizon are judged as of the run date, and each technique records the sources the judgment rests on.
