## 8. Where the techniques land

### 8.1 How techniques change mechanics

Each `affects` edge records one of seven effects. Across {{stat:n_affects_edges}} edges the most common effect is *deepens* ({{stat:n_effect_deepens}} edges): the technique makes an existing mechanic richer, larger, or more varied without changing its role.{{assert:top_effect == Deepens}} Within this atlas, explanation is the rarest coded effect ({{stat:n_effect_explains}} edges).{{assert:rarest_effect == Explains}}

{{table:effect-distribution}}

**Coding rule.** An edge carries one effect, the primary one: the change its note describes to what the mechanic can be. When more than one fits, the first that applies in this order is used. *Replaces substrate* when the technique swaps how the mechanic computes its state while its role in play stays. *Enables new form* when it makes possible a version the old implementation could not produce at any cost. *Explains* when its output is addressed to the player's understanding of the mechanic's state or causes. *Deepens* when the same mechanic gains fidelity, scale, or variety at runtime. *Accelerates* when the same output costs less runtime compute. For work done at design time, *cuts authoring cost* when it makes the content the mechanic consumes, and *tunes* when it sets or tests the mechanic's parameters. The order was written down after the edges were coded, by the same rater, to state the convention the edges were meant to follow.

**Precedence audit.** That rater then read every edge's note against the order and recorded whether the label meets it and any second label the note also supports (`report/audits/effect-audit.yaml`). As first coded, {{stat:n_effect_audit_compliant}} of {{stat:n_effect_audit_edges}} labels ({{stat:pct_effect_audit_compliant}} percent) meet it. The audit corrected {{stat:n_effect_audit_corrected}} labels that their notes do not support or that a label earlier in the order plainly fits, most often a deepening whose note says the old rule could not produce the new form ({{stat:n_effect_audit_deepens_to_enables_new}} edges).{{assert:effect_audit_top_correction_from == Deepens}}{{assert:effect_audit_top_correction_to == Enables new form}} It left {{stat:n_effect_audit_left}} for a second rater, and all but {{stat:n_effect_audit_left_other}} of them sit on one boundary.{{assert:n_effect_audit_boundary > n_effect_audit_corrected}} Their notes say the technique computes the mechanic in place of a named rule, as a learned model of flow replaces lift and drag tables, and also that the mechanic gets richer or cheaper. The edges were coded deepens or accelerates for the richer or cheaper behavior, while the written order puts replaces substrate first. The disagreement is systematic, so it is a question about which of the two states the convention, not a set of slips, and those labels stay as coded. Applied to the letter, the order would make replaces substrate the most common effect ({{stat:n_effect_replaces_substrate_by_letter}} edges against {{stat:n_effect_deepens_by_letter}} deepening) and acceleration the rarest.{{assert:top_effect_by_letter == Replaces substrate}}{{assert:rarest_effect_by_letter == Accelerates}}{{assert:n_effect_deepens_by_letter < n_effect_replaces_substrate_by_letter}} The family order by exposure (§8.2) would barely move (ρ = {{stat:rho_family_exposure_by_letter}} with the stored labels), but agents and behavior would lead it, as it does with equal weights.{{assert:rho_family_exposure_by_letter >= 0.9}}{{assert:top_exposed_family_by_letter == Agents & behavior}}{{assert:top_exposed_family_equal == Agents & behavior}} The same rater coded the edges, wrote the order, and made the audit, so the audit tests whether the labels follow the written rules, not whether another reader would choose them; that waits for an independent coder (§11.1).

A count this small and this concentrated describes the atlas more than the field. Two features of the method push it down: a technique enters only if a result changes a named game mechanic (§7.1), so general explainability research (attribution, causal tracing, question answering over logs) is absent unless someone has applied it to a game; and an edge carries one label, so a technique that explains while it replaces a substrate or enables a new form is coded by the earlier label, which the second labels above only partly recover. Scarcity in the record may also reflect low demand for explanation, or its difficulty. What the count does show is that the recorded edges concentrate on generating content and replacing substrates (rule-based behavior with language models, numerical physics with learned dynamics), and that few recorded results address explaining a simulation to the person playing it, although the components that would need (retrieval over structured logs, constrained generation, summarization) exist.

### 8.2 Exposure by family

Technique exposure is a deployment-weighted heuristic. For each mechanic it sums, over the techniques with an `affects` edge to it, the technique's maturity weight times the edge's effect weight; a family's exposure is the sum over its mechanics, and its exposure per mechanic divides that by the number of mechanics. Maturity weights run from {{stat:w_exposure_maturity_min}} for research to {{stat:w_exposure_maturity_max}} for a genre norm, so the index measures where deployable results point and down-weights early-stage work by construction. Effect weights run from {{stat:w_exposure_effect_min}} to {{stat:w_exposure_effect_max}}, counting a change to what a mechanic can be above tuning it or speeding it up. ### 8.5 Authoritative state versus presentation

The techniques divide by whether they touch authoritative simulation state. Those that change only presentation have shipped (neural upscaling and frame generation, asset generation, motion synthesis) or reached prototypes (restyling); they preserve determinism or can be made reproducible by storing what they generate.{{assert:field.real-time-video-restyling.maturity == prototype}} Removing the two whose records say they never touch state barely moves the family order in total (ρ = {{stat:rho_family_exposure_no_presentation}}).{{assert:rho_family_exposure_no_presentation >= 0.95}} Those that would replace physics, fields, or the world with learned prediction remain prototypes or research in games. The shipped exceptions that reach state are narrow and controlled: cached model outputs as rules ([[llm-rule-adjudication]]), language-model characters that choose actions in [[inzoi]] ([[generative-agents]], [[on-device-language-models]]), and opponents ([[deep-rl-npc-policies]], [[monte-carlo-tree-search]]). Of these, only the language characters break reproducibility outright.{{assert:field.generative-agents.determinism_impact == breaks}}{{assert:field.deep-rl-npc-policies.determinism_impact == mitigable}}{{assert:field.monte-carlo-tree-search.determinism_impact == mitigable}}{{assert:field.llm-rule-adjudication.determinism_impact == mitigable}} The pattern suggests a design principle that recurs through §9: keep the simulation symbolic, typed, and logged, and let learned models read it, explain it, decorate it, and propose changes to it through validated interfaces.
