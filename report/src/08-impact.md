## 8. Where the techniques land

### 8.1 How techniques change mechanics

Each `affects` edge records one of seven effects. Across {{stat:n_affects_edges}} edges the most common effect is *replaces substrate* ({{stat:n_effect_replaces_substrate}} edges): the technique computes the mechanic in place of its existing rule while the mechanic keeps its role in play, and often makes it richer as it does.{{assert:top_effect == Replaces substrate}} Within this atlas, acceleration is the rarest coded effect ({{stat:n_effect_accelerates}} edges) and explanation the next rarest ({{stat:n_effect_explains}}).{{assert:rarest_effect == Accelerates}}{{assert:rank_effect_explains == 2}}

{{table:effect-distribution}}

**Coding rule.** An edge carries one effect, the primary one: the change its note describes to what the mechanic can be. When more than one fits, the first that applies in this order is used. *Replaces substrate* when the technique swaps how the mechanic computes its state while its role in play stays. *Enables new form* when it makes possible a version the old implementation could not produce at any cost. *Explains* when its output is addressed to the player's understanding of the mechanic's state or causes. *Deepens* when the same mechanic gains fidelity, scale, or variety at runtime. *Accelerates* when the same output costs less runtime compute. For work done at design time, *cuts authoring cost* when it makes the content the mechanic consumes, and *tunes* when it sets or tests the mechanic's parameters. The order was written down after the edges were coded, by the same rater, to state the convention the edges were meant to follow.

**Precedence audit.** That rater then read every edge's note against the order and recorded whether the label met it and any second label the note also supports (`report/audits/effect-audit.yaml`). As first coded, {{stat:n_effect_audit_compliant}} of {{stat:n_effect_audit_edges}} labels ({{stat:pct_effect_audit_compliant}} percent) met it. A count this small and this concentrated describes the atlas more than the field. Two features of the method push it down: a technique enters only if a result changes a named game mechanic (§7.1), so general explainability research (attribution, causal tracing, question answering over logs) is absent unless someone has applied it to a game; and an edge carries one label, so a technique that explains while it replaces a substrate or enables a new form is coded by the earlier label, which the second labels above only partly recover. Scarcity in the record may also reflect low demand for explanation, or its difficulty. What the count does show is that the recorded edges concentrate on generating content and replacing substrates (rule-based behavior with language models, numerical physics with learned dynamics), and that few recorded results address explaining a simulation to the person playing it, although the components that would need (retrieval over structured logs, constrained generation, summarization) exist.

### 8.2 Exposure by family

Technique exposure is a deployment-weighted heuristic. For each mechanic it sums, over the techniques with an `affects` edge to it, the technique's maturity weight times the edge's effect weight; a family's exposure is the sum over its mechanics, and its exposure per mechanic divides that by the number of mechanics. The swept mechanics carry {{stat:pct_unreached_uses_swept}} percent of the uses on mechanics that had no edge, a keyword finds only a change described in words the list anticipated, the same rater wrote the edges and judged the hits, and a result the atlas never cites cannot be found this way. Recall against results outside the cited set needs the independent derivation of edges set out in §11.1.

Many unreached mechanics also have a technique proposed for them by an opportunity (§9). In {{stat:n_opportunity_pairs_unlinked}} of the {{stat:n_opportunity_mechanic_pairs}} pairs of an opportunity and a mechanic it involves, none of the opportunity's own techniques has an edge to that mechanic yet; counting a mechanic as reached once an opportunity proposes a technique for it leaves {{stat:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure_unproposed}} percent of uses unreached, against {{stat:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure}} percent on published edges alone.{{assert:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure_unproposed < pct_uses_on_zero_exposure}}{{assert:n_opportunity_pairs_unlinked > 0}} The larger figure measures where recorded results have not gone; the smaller one, where this atlas has not yet proposed anything. The same rater wrote the proposals.

In this atlas, then, recorded technique edges concentrate in the agent, narrative, control, and physics families and leave much of the economic core unreached. A contrast between what games show and say and what they are built from would overstate this. The leading families mostly own simulation state by the classes of §2.1, the family-level proxy disagrees with those classes on {{stat:n_state_class_proxy_keeps_nonstate}} mechanics, and on simulation-state mechanics alone the unreached share of uses barely moves ({{stat:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure_state_class}} percent against {{stat:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure}}).{{assert:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure_state_class_diff > -5}}{{assert:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure_state_class_diff < 5}} How far that describes AI research rather than the atlas's selection of it is open, since the set was gathered by searching for results that name game mechanics (§7.1). If the pattern holds for the field, it is partly sensible (production chains and budgets are cheap and legible already) and partly a gap: the economic core is where balance work, legibility problems, and authoring of recipes and scenarios consume design time.

### 8.5 Authoritative state versus presentation

The techniques divide by whether they touch authoritative simulation state. Those that change only presentation have shipped (neural upscaling and frame generation, asset generation, motion synthesis) or reached prototypes (restyling); they preserve determinism or can be made reproducible by storing what they generate.{{assert:field.real-time-video-restyling.maturity == prototype}} Removing the two whose records say they never touch state barely moves the family order in total (ρ = {{stat:rho_family_exposure_no_presentation}}).{{assert:rho_family_exposure_no_presentation >= 0.95}} Those that would replace physics, fields, or the world with learned prediction remain prototypes or research in games. The shipped exceptions that reach state are narrow and controlled: cached model outputs as rules ([[llm-rule-adjudication]]), language-model characters that choose actions in [[inzoi]] ([[generative-agents]], [[on-device-language-models]]), and opponents ([[deep-rl-npc-policies]], [[monte-carlo-tree-search]]). Of these, only the language characters break reproducibility outright.{{assert:field.generative-agents.determinism_impact == breaks}}{{assert:field.deep-rl-npc-policies.determinism_impact == mitigable}}{{assert:field.monte-carlo-tree-search.determinism_impact == mitigable}}{{assert:field.llm-rule-adjudication.determinism_impact == mitigable}} The pattern suggests a design principle that recurs through §9: keep the simulation symbolic, typed, and logged, and let learned models read it, explain it, decorate it, and propose changes to it through validated interfaces.
