## Abstract

Simulation games are built from a shared but undocumented vocabulary of rule-systems. This report makes that vocabulary explicit and machine-readable, then asks how mathematical, AI, ML, and LLM results from 2019 to 2026 change it. We decompose {{count:game}} simulation games ({{stat:year_min}} to {{stat:year_max}}, {{stat:n_subgenres}} subgenres) into {{count:mechanic}} atomic mechanics, analyze each mechanic on its own (definition, levers, failure modes, dynamics, mathematical basis, and six anchored scores), map {{count:technique}} techniques onto mechanics through {{stat:n_affects_edges}} typed effect edges, and synthesize {{count:opportunity}} design opportunities, each with a validation experiment and a dated, falsifiable prediction. All results are projections of a versioned dataset with provenance for every entity and a validator that keeps the report, wiki, tables, and graph consistent.

**Findings.**

1. **Simulation games are compositions of six layers**: substrate, dynamics, coupling, control, legibility, and pacing. Clustering games by the mechanics they use, with the number of clusters chosen by resampling stability rather than by eye, recovers design families (minds, managed crowds, planned economies, build-and-test, hands-on work, authored decisions, generated worlds) that cut across market subgenres; {{stat:n_clusters_cross_subgenre}} of {{stat:n_clusters}} clusters mix subgenres, and {{stat:n_stable_clusters}} survive resampling.{{assert:n_clusters_cross_subgenre >= 10}}{{assert:n_stable_clusters >= 10}}
2. **Later games are built mostly from old parts.** {{stat:pct_mechanics_seen_by_2000}} percent of mechanics appear in a catalog game released in or before 2000,{{assert:pct_mechanics_seen_by_2000 >= 60}} but random sets of the same number of games from any period cover nearly as many (p = {{stat:p_mechanics_seen_by_2000}}), so that share mostly reflects how widely mechanics are shared rather than early invention.{{assert:p_mechanics_seen_by_2000 > 0.05}} Reuse is the finding that holds: games released since 2010 draw {{stat:pct_uses_since_2010_on_pre2001}} percent of their mechanic uses from mechanics already seen in or before 2000.{{assert:pct_uses_since_2010_on_pre2001 >= 75}} {{stat:n_mechanics_since_2010}} mechanics first appear in 2010 or later, and {{stat:pair_coverage_pct}} percent of possible mechanic pairs have ever co-occurred.
3. **Deeper mechanics are rated harder to read.** On rubric scores from a single model rater, of which an independent second rater has so far scored {{stat:n_scores_independently_rated}} of {{stat:n_mechanic_scores}} ({{stat:pct_mechanic_scores_independently_rated}} percent),{{assert:pct_mechanic_scores_independently_rated < 50}} legibility falls as emergence potential rises (ρ = {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential}}) and as compute cost rises (ρ = {{stat:rho_legibility_compute_cost}}); emergence rises with tuning difficulty (ρ = {{stat:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty}}).{{assert:rho_legibility_emergence_potential < -0.2}}{{assert:rho_legibility_compute_cost < 0}}{{assert:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty > 0.2}}
4. **AI techniques target what games show and say, not what they are built from.** Exposure concentrates in narrative, control, agent, and physics mechanics.{{assert:top_exposed_family == Narrative & generation}} {{stat:n_mechanics_zero_exposure}} mechanics, carrying {{stat:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure}} percent of all mechanic uses in the catalog, are affected by no technique in the set. Explanation is the rarest effect a technique has on a mechanic ({{stat:n_effect_explains}} of {{stat:n_affects_edges}} edges).{{assert:rarest_effect == Explains}}
5. **What shipped mostly stays at the edge of simulation state.** Neural rendering, asset and motion generation, and language characters for dialogue and companionship have shipped. The shipped techniques that write into game state are narrow: cached model outputs as crafting rules, language-model characters in a few titles, and learned or search-based opponents. Frame-generating world models and learned physics remain prototypes or research.{{assert:n_technique_shipped_mainstream <= 2}}{{assert:field.genie-world-models.maturity == prototype}}{{assert:field.subspace-neural-physics.maturity == prototype}}{{assert:field.graph-network-simulators.maturity == research}} Of the {{stat:n_technique_runtime}} techniques that can run during play, {{stat:n_technique_runtime_breaks}} break reproducibility outright.
6. **The best near-term opportunities keep the simulation symbolic** and let learned models read, explain, decorate, and propose through validated, logged interfaces. The highest-priority opportunity is {{stat:top_opportunity}}.
