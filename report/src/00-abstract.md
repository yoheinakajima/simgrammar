## Abstract

Simulation games are built from a shared but undocumented vocabulary of rule-systems. Naming it shows designers which combinations are crowded and which are empty, what each part costs in computation, writing, and clarity, and where new techniques could change a game. This report makes that vocabulary explicit and machine-readable, then asks how AI, machine-learning, language-model, and simulation results from 2019 to 2026, and {{stat:n_technique_pre_window}} older techniques they build on, change it. "Grammar" here means a shared, machine-readable vocabulary of rule-systems with typed relations, not a set of production rules. We decompose {{count:game}} simulation games ({{stat:year_min}} to {{stat:year_max}}, {{stat:n_subgenres}} subgenres) into {{count:mechanic}} atomic mechanics, analyze each mechanic on its own (definition, levers, failure modes, dynamics, mathematical basis, and six anchored scores), map {{count:technique}} techniques onto mechanics through {{stat:n_affects_edges}} `affects` edges, each typed by how the technique changes the mechanic, and synthesize {{count:opportunity}} design opportunities, each with a validation experiment and a dated, falsifiable prediction. All results come from an open, versioned dataset: every technique and {{stat:n_game_sourced_or_verified}} of the {{count:game}} games are sourced or verified, every game cites at least one source, and {{stat:n_mechanic_asserted}} of the {{count:mechanic}} mechanics and every opportunity remain asserted.{{assert:n_technique_asserted == 0}}{{assert:n_game_no_sources == 0}}{{assert:n_opportunity_asserted == n_opportunity}} Numbers reach the prose only through tokens the build fills in from the data, and {{stat:n_report_assertions}} assertions fail the build when the data stop supporting a ranking, sign, threshold, or grouping the text states; whether an interpretation is valid is not checked. We organize the analysis by six proposed layers (substrate, dynamics, coupling, control, legibility, and pacing); the dataset does not tag layers, and none of the findings below tests them.

**Findings.**

1. **Mechanics group games into families that cut across subgenre labels.** Clustering games by the mechanics they use, with the number of clusters fixed by a resampling rule rather than by eye, gives a {{stat:cluster_k}}-way partition, one admissible cut among its neighbours. {{stat:n_groupings_paper_agreed}} of the {{stat:n_groupings_paper}} families the paper names (among them life sims, colony sims, tycoons, factory games, grand strategy, and vehicle sims) hold together in at least {{stat:cocluster_together_pct}} percent of resamplings and under a second clustering method; the links that would join them into broader groups do not. Agreement with subgenre labels, corrected for chance, is adjusted mutual information {{stat:cluster_subgenre_ami}} (shuffled labels give {{stat:cluster_subgenre_null_ami}}) and stays between {{stat:cluster_subgenre_ami_near_min}} and {{stat:cluster_subgenre_ami_near_max}} from k = {{stat:cluster_k_near_min}} to {{stat:cluster_k}}.{{assert:n_groupings_paper_agreed >= 18}}{{assert:cluster_subgenre_ami > 0.2}}{{assert:cluster_subgenre_ami < 0.8}}{{assert:cluster_subgenre_ami_near_min > 0.4}}
2. **Later games are built mostly from old parts, as the catalog's makeup predicts.** {{stat:pct_mechanics_seen_by_2000}} percent of mechanics appear in a catalog game released in or before 2000,{{assert:pct_mechanics_seen_by_2000 >= 60}} but random sets of later games with as many mechanic uses cover slightly more ({{stat:n_mechanics_seen_null_uses_mean}} against {{stat:n_mechanics_seen_by_2000}}; p = {{stat:p_mechanics_seen_uses}}), so that share reflects how widely mechanics are shared, not an early period of invention.{{assert:n_mechanics_seen_null_uses_mean > n_mechanics_seen_by_2000}}{{assert:p_mechanics_seen_uses > 0.05}} Games released since 2010 draw {{stat:pct_uses_since_2010_on_pre2001}} percent of their mechanic uses from mechanics seen by 2000, against {{stat:pct_uses_since_2010_on_pre2001_null_mean}} percent when release years are shuffled.{{assert:pct_uses_since_2010_on_pre2001 >= 75}}{{assert:p_uses_since_2010_on_pre2001 > 0.05}} {{stat:n_mechanics_since_2010}} mechanics are first observed in 2010 or later, a count that depends on where lineages enter the sample. {{stat:pair_coverage_pct}} percent of possible mechanic pairs have co-occurred, fewer than the {{stat:pair_coverage_null_mean_pct}} percent a size-preserving null gives (p = {{stat:p_pair_coverage}}), consistent with lineages reusing the same pairs; the rest are unobserved in this sample.{{assert:pair_coverage_pct < pair_coverage_null_mean_pct}}{{assert:p_pair_coverage < 0.05}}
3. **Mechanics rated higher for emergence potential are rated harder to read.** In rubric scores assigned mostly by one model rater, mechanics rated as generating more unscripted variety when coupled with others are rated less legible (ρ = {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential}}; {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_family_low}} to {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_family_high}} with families resampled) and harder to balance (ρ = {{stat:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty}}), and mechanics rated costlier to run are rated less legible (ρ = {{stat:rho_legibility_compute_cost}}).{{assert:rho_legibility_emergence_potential < -0.2}}{{assert:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_family_high < 0}}{{assert:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty > 0.2}}{{assert:rho_legibility_compute_cost < 0}} These are exploratory rubric-consistency findings, not measurements of play. An independent second rater has scored {{stat:n_scores_independently_rated}} of the {{stat:n_mechanic_scores}} scores, from {{stat:n_mechanics_independently_rated}} mechanics;{{assert:pct_mechanic_scores_independently_rated < 50}} on those the legibility–emergence correlation is ρ = {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_second_rater}} on its scores and {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_original_rater}} on the original's, neither distinguishable from zero at that size, while on the {{stat:n_calibration_mechanics}} calibration mechanics its scores give ρ = {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_calibration_second}}.{{assert:p_legibility_emergence_potential_second_rater > 0.05}}{{assert:p_legibility_emergence_potential_original_rater > 0.05}}{{assert:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_calibration_second < -0.5}} Interface mechanics sit at the floor of emergence potential by construction ({{stat:pct_interface_emergence_1}} percent score 1).{{assert:pct_interface_emergence_1 >= 50}}
4. **In this atlas, recorded technique edges concentrate on what games show and say, not on what they are built from.** Exposure, a deployment-weighted count of `affects` edges, concentrates in narrative, agent, control, and physics mechanics under equal, maturity-only, and effect-only weights alike (family rank ρ ≥ {{stat:min_rho_family_exposure_weights}}), though which family leads changes.{{assert:top_exposed_family == Narrative & generation}}{{assert:min_rho_family_exposure_weights >= 0.9}} {{stat:n_mechanics_zero_exposure}} of the {{count:mechanic}} mechanics, carrying {{stat:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure}} percent of mechanic uses ({{stat:pct_core_uses_on_zero_exposure}} percent of core uses), have no recorded technique edge; counting the mechanics an opportunity proposes a technique for leaves {{stat:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure_unproposed}} percent.{{assert:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure_unproposed < pct_uses_on_zero_exposure}} One rater wrote the techniques, edges, and proposals. Explanation is the rarest coded effect ({{stat:n_effect_explains}} of {{stat:n_affects_edges}} edges, {{stat:n_effect_explains_top_technique}} of them from one technique).{{assert:rarest_effect == Explains}}
5. **What shipped mostly stays at the edge of simulation state.** Neural rendering, asset and motion generation, and language characters for dialogue and companionship have shipped. The shipped techniques that write into game state are narrow: cached model outputs as crafting rules, language-model characters in a few titles, and learned or search-based opponents. Frame-generating world models and learned physics remain prototypes or research.{{assert:n_technique_shipped_mainstream <= 2}}{{assert:field.genie-world-models.maturity == prototype}}{{assert:field.subspace-neural-physics.maturity == prototype}}{{assert:field.graph-network-simulators.maturity == research}} Of the {{stat:n_technique_runtime}} techniques that can run during play, {{stat:n_technique_runtime_breaks}} break reproducibility outright.
6. **The best near-term opportunities keep the simulation symbolic** and let learned models read, explain, decorate, and propose through validated, logged interfaces.
