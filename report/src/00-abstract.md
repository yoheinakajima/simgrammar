## Abstract

Simulation games are built from a shared vocabulary of rule-systems that designers use every day and no one has written down. This report writes it down as an open, machine-readable atlas, then uses it to find where AI, machine-learning, language-model, and simulation results from 2019 to 2026, and {{stat:n_technique_pre_window}} older techniques they build on, change what those rule-systems can do. We decompose {{count:game}} simulation games ({{stat:year_min}} to {{stat:year_max}}, {{stat:n_subgenres}} subgenres) into {{count:mechanic}} atomic mechanics; analyze each mechanic's definition, levers, failure modes, dynamics, mathematical basis, and six anchored scores; map {{count:technique}} techniques onto mechanics through {{stat:n_affects_edges}} `affects` edges, each typed by how the technique changes the mechanic; and derive {{count:opportunity}} design opportunities, each with a validation experiment and a dated, falsifiable prediction. Every technique and every game cites its sources.{{assert:n_technique_asserted == 0}}{{assert:n_game_no_sources == 0}} Every number in the text is filled in from the dataset at build time, and {{stat:n_report_assertions}} assertions fail the build when the data stop supporting a ranking, sign, threshold, or grouping the text states.

**Findings.**

1. **Games group by what they are built from, and the groups cut across genre labels.** Clustering games by their mechanics, with the number of clusters set by a resampling rule, gives a {{stat:cluster_k}}-way partition. Each of the {{stat:n_groupings_paper}} families named in it (life sims, colony sims, tycoons, factory games, grand strategy, vehicle sims, and others) is named by two to four central games, and the named games of {{stat:n_groupings_paper_agreed}} share a cluster in at least {{stat:cocluster_together_pct}} percent of resamplings and under a second clustering method; tested whole, {{stat:n_stable_clusters}} of the {{stat:n_clusters}} clusters meet Hennig's stability mark. The partition matches subgenre labels only in part (adjusted mutual information {{stat:cluster_subgenre_ami}}, {{stat:cluster_subgenre_ami_near_min}} to {{stat:cluster_subgenre_ami_near_max}} at every cut the resampling rule admits; shuffled labels give {{stat:cluster_subgenre_null_ami}}).{{assert:n_groupings_paper_agreed >= 18}}{{assert:cluster_subgenre_ami > 0.2}}{{assert:cluster_subgenre_ami < 0.8}}
2. **New games are assembled from old parts, in conservative combinations.** {{stat:pct_mechanics_seen_by_2000}} percent of the vocabulary was in a catalog game by 2000, and games released since 2010 draw {{stat:pct_uses_since_2010_on_pre2001}} percent of their mechanic uses from those parts.{{assert:pct_mechanics_seen_by_2000 >= 60}}{{assert:pct_uses_since_2010_on_pre2001 >= 75}} In the catalog, {{stat:pair_coverage_pct}} percent of possible mechanic pairs share a game, against {{stat:pair_coverage_null_mean_pct}} percent under a null that keeps every game's size and every mechanic's frequency (p {{stat:p_pair_coverage_shown}}), and {{stat:pair_coverage_across_families_pct}} against {{stat:pair_coverage_across_families_null_mean_pct}} percent across families, where no pair is exclusive by design: games reuse the same pairs, and the pairs of common mechanics that never meet are the white space of §5.5.{{assert:pair_coverage_pct < pair_coverage_null_mean_pct}}{{assert:p_pair_coverage < 0.05}}{{assert:pair_coverage_across_families_pct < pair_coverage_across_families_null_lo_pct}}
3. **The more variety a mechanic generates, the harder it is to read.** Mechanics rated higher for emergence potential are rated less legible (ρ = {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential}}; {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_family_low}} to {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_family_high}} with families resampled, {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_within_family}} within families) and harder to tune (ρ = {{stat:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty}}), and a second model scoring a random sample blind gives the same sign, weaker (ρ = {{stat:xm_rho_legibility_emergence_potential_second}}, {{stat:xm_rho_legibility_emergence_potential_second_low}} to {{stat:xm_rho_legibility_emergence_potential_second_high}}).{{assert:rho_legibility_emergence_potential < -0.2}}{{assert:rho_legibility_emergence_potential_family_high < 0}}{{assert:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty > 0.2}}{{assert:xm_rho_legibility_emergence_potential_second < 0}} This depth-for-legibility trade is the ceiling that explanation techniques target (§6.3, §9).
4. **AI techniques reach agents, narrative, control, and physics, and leave half of what games use untouched.** Technique edges concentrate in those four families under every weighting tested (family rank ρ ≥ {{stat:min_rho_family_exposure_weights}}). {{stat:n_mechanics_zero_exposure}} of the {{count:mechanic}} mechanics, carrying {{stat:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure}} percent of all mechanic uses, have no technique edge.{{assert:top_exposed_family == Agents & behavior}}{{assert:min_rho_family_exposure_weights >= 0.9}}{{assert:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure > 40}}{{assert:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure < 60}} Under the precedence rule of §8.1, replacing a mechanic's substrate is the most common effect ({{stat:n_effect_replaces_substrate}} edges, against {{stat:n_effect_deepens_before_audit}} that deepen it, the most common as first coded){{assert:top_effect_before_audit == Deepens}}; explaining a mechanic is the second rarest ({{stat:n_effect_explains}} of {{stat:n_affects_edges}} edges).{{assert:top_effect == Replaces substrate}}{{assert:rank_effect_explains == 2}}
5. **Shipped AI stays at the edge of simulation state.** Neural rendering, asset and motion generation, and language-model characters for dialogue have shipped. The shipped techniques that write into game state are narrow: cached model outputs as crafting rules, language-model characters in a few titles, and learned or search-based opponents. Frame-generating world models and learned physics are prototypes or research.{{assert:n_technique_shipped_mainstream <= 2}}{{assert:field.genie-world-models.maturity == prototype}}{{assert:field.subspace-neural-physics.maturity == prototype}}{{assert:field.graph-network-simulators.maturity == research}} {{stat:n_technique_runtime_breaks}} of the {{stat:n_technique_runtime}} techniques that run during play break re-simulation from inputs and lockstep multiplayer; logging their outputs restores replay of a recorded session.
6. **The top-ranked near-term bets keep the simulation symbolic and let models explain it.** The opportunities are written to one design position: the simulation stays symbolic, and learned models read, explain, decorate, and propose through validated, logged interfaces. The top-ranked one is {{stat:top_opportunity}}: it answers a player's "why did that happen?" from the game's own event log. It ranks first under the stored weights, equal weights, and mean ranks, stays in the top {{stat:opportunity_top_n}} under every random draw of weights, and drops to second only when a model called during play with no stated budget is capped on feasibility. The ranking is the original rater's: a second model's blind scores order the opportunities differently (ρ = {{stat:xm_rho_priority}}).{{assert:xm_rho_priority < 0.5}}{{assert:top_opportunity == Why-query over the simulation log}}{{assert:rank_top_opportunity_equal_weights == 1}}{{assert:rank_top_opportunity_mean_rank == 1}}{{assert:top_share_dirichlet_top_opportunity == 100}}{{assert:rank_top_opportunity_cost_capped == 2}} Each of the {{stat:n_predictions}} predictions carries a reference class, a diffusion baseline, and a catalog base rate fixed before any resolves, and §11 fixes in advance how the set as a whole will be scored.

**Reliability.** A second model, run by the same operator, coded and scored blind samples of every decision. It agrees with the stored families, simulation-state classes, and technique maturity (κ = {{stat:xm_kappa_family}}, {{stat:xm_kappa_state}}, {{stat:xm_kappa_maturity}}), less on effect types and horizons (κ = {{stat:xm_kappa_effect}}, {{stat:xm_kappa_horizon}}), on admission in {{stat:xm_pct_agree_admission}} percent of items (κ = {{stat:xm_kappa_admission}}, low because nearly every candidate passes), and least on the value judgments behind the opportunity ranking (ordinal alpha {{stat:xm_alpha_opportunity_min}} to {{stat:xm_alpha_opportunity_max}}).{{assert:xm_kappa_family >= 0.8}}{{assert:xm_kappa_state >= 0.7}}{{assert:xm_kappa_maturity >= 0.8}}{{assert:xm_kappa_effect < xm_kappa_family}}{{assert:xm_kappa_horizon < xm_kappa_family}}{{assert:xm_alpha_opportunity_max < 0.5}} §2.6 sets out the limits of the sample and the ratings. The dataset, schemas, contract, and every projection are open for extension, and `AGENTS.md` gives the procedure for contributors, human or agent.
